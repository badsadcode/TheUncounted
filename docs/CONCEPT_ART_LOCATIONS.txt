THE UNCOUNTED: houses and points of interest, for concept art

Overall feel: a dark fantasy borderland, with bureaucratic absurdity on top of
real grief. Timber-framed villages, old stone, fog and lantern light. It has
rained on Bellwether for seventeen years. Names have power here, so erased,
chewed-out and scratched-off lettering is a recurring motif.


BELLWETHER VILLAGE (the walled home village; rain, cobbles, lantern light)
 1. Records Office: the Royal Bureau of Records' local office. Ledgers, shelves,
    a clerk's desk, a stamp that decides whether you exist.
 2. The Lantern Inn: warm hearth, stew, a window seat facing the door, and the
    table where the Thursday game is played.
 3. Vaelen's Provisions: a quartermaster's shop and market stall, crates, a
    workbench.
 4. Dawn Chapel: Church of the Last Dawn, a golden altar, and a horse standing
    on the roof ridge that nobody can explain.
 5. Municipal Library: Fenwick's domain, tall shelves, and a history aisle that
    is "closed for reshelving". (Shares a building with the chapel at present.)
 6. The Village Square: the well where Old Tobble tells tales, the census board
    with one name scraped off, big enough for a sleeping dragon.
 7. The Town Walls and East Gate: mossy stone, torches, an archway to the road.


THE OLD ROAD (a misty forest road leading east)
 8. Boundary Stone of Oakhallow: a tall monolith with a town's name on it, and
    no town. Only forest, where 412 people used to live.
 9. The Mid-Road Arch: a lone stone arch standing over the road.
10. Kaelen's Camp: a scout's camp with a field journal, lit by a lantern.
11. The Figure It Out Wagon: a flower-painted mystery-solving wagon and its camp.
12. The Toll That Isn't: a hand-painted "HEDLESS KNIGHT" sign beside the road.
13. The Annex Arch: a ruined archway and torch-lit pillars at the cliff, with
    stairs going down.
14. The Dusk Milestone: a marker on a road that is only there after dusk.


REGISTRY ANNEX & FORGOTTEN CRYPT (underground, beneath the road)
15. The Foyer: a sunken government office, with a desk still waiting for the
    public.
16. The Archive Hall: pillars, bookshelves and an iron wall-lever; ledgers that
    outlasted the people in them.
17. The Royal Vault: sealed behind a gate that opens with a rune plate.
18. The Forgotten Crypt: older stone than the building above it, a sarcophagus,
    and a pale blue glow.
19. The Sanctum of the Uncounted: rune-lit monoliths around the lectern that
    holds the Uncounted Ledger.


VAULT III (deeper still)
20. The Nursery: a pillared hall of small sarcophagi under clouded glass, with
    forty sleeping children and blue ghost-flames. Quiet and tender, not scary.
21. The Seal Chamber: a very tall chamber, an altar, tall glowing monoliths, and
    the Seal of Names.


HOLLOWMERE (a haunted manor, played for laughs)
22. The Lawn of Ill-Advised Choices: a graveyard of adventuring-party mistakes,
    with a cheerful sign.
23. The Grounds and Rolf's Garden: a long drive, a gate, vegetable beds.
24. Hollowmere Manor, exterior: a grand, two-storey country house.
25. The Foyer: a grand staircase and a suspicious figure in the hall.
26. The Dining Room: table settings for guests who are long gone.
27. The Library: towering shelves, and a "Most Borrowed" bestseller stand.
28. The Kitchen: the back door that is always open.
29. The Ballroom: a stage, a gramophone, a cold patch on the floor.
30. The Portrait Gallery: a row of family portraits that look back.
31. The Master Bedroom, the Locked Study, the Sealed Room: decanters, an open
    journal, a spirit trumpet and a secret lever.


SALTMERE DOCKS (a fog-bound harbour with something enormous underneath)
32. The Quay and Piers: wooden piers, boats, shallow water you can wade through,
    and giant tentacles out in the fog.
33. The Drowned Lantern: a dockside tavern. "Patrons are reminded not to say
    their names on the premises." Mother Brine's pot, Pickering's back room.
34. The Harbourmaster's Post: a small lookout covered in nervous notices.
35. Gale & Sons' Warehouse: crates, shipping ledgers and a gated cellar stair.
36. The Sunken Chapel: a flooded, wrong-feeling place beneath the warehouse, with
    tide bells and hymns carved in the wet stone.


THE WHISTLING WOOD (a pine forest to the west)
37. The Old Oak: one huge ancient tree with a whistling knot-hole.
38. Grandmother's Cottage: ruined, with a note on the bed. Wolves may be involved.
39. Steve's Camp: a tent and a campfire.
40. Coth's Camp: a tiny fire, Sir Pebbleton the painted stone on his log, and 72
    tally marks cut into a pine.
41. The Pond: a quiet fishing spot, and the walled lane back to the village.


REACHED BY BOAT
42. The Sleeper's Reef and the Saltmere Light: a drowned lighthouse on an island
    past the fog, with a keeper who never leaves, sleeping tentacles, and bottles
    holding names.
43. The Late Cindermaw: a kobold lawyer's cliffside mansion, bought with a
    dragon's pension. A jetty and a cove, 2,104 gold's worth of cushions and no
    chairs, a shoe room of tiny shoes, and a vault.


HERE: THE CITY FORMERLY KNOWN AS (a forgotten city; open dusk to dawn)
44. The City Gate and Customs Booth: gate towers, a fog bank and a desk where
    nobody has come in for a very long time.
45. The Dry Fountain: "WELCOME TO" and then a gouge, as if something bit the name
    out of the stone.
46. The Streets and Houses: 41 houses and 47 hearths. Seen from above, the streets
    spell something.
47. The Ruined Bakery: the building with three hearths.
48. The Midnight Market: open midnight until two. Sells lost umbrellas and one
    Tuesday. Names are not for sale.
49. The Cartographer's House: maps of a place nobody can name.
50. The Bell Tower: a tall tower with a parapet looking out over the moonlit city.
51. The Archive of Here: a brass plaque chewed like a pencil end, and the plinth
    where the name is kept.


THE AUDIT CHAMBER (a Bureau challenge arena)
52. A grand, intimidating hall of banners, pillars and a raised dais, where
    existence is examined in ten stages.
