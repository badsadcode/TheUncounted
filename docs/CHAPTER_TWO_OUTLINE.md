# Chapter Two: The Town Where It Is Always Tuesday (outline for approval)

## The hook

Six struck cities remain. The second wasn't hidden by taking its name: the Archivists hid it by taking its **date**.
The Hunger can't find a place that never reaches tomorrow, so the town was folded into a single day and left there.
Four hundred years later, it is still the same Tuesday.

You've already seen the way in: the Night Merchant in Here sells **"One Tuesday, slightly used"**. After Chapter One,
the merchant offers it to you (it's not for sale, it's a delivery: "it's been waiting for someone uncounted"). Use it
at dusk, and you step into **Wednesbury**, where it is 9 in the morning on a Tuesday, as it always is.

## The place: Wednesbury

A market town on a river, bright, busy, and wrong:

- **The day repeats.** The game's clock runs from 9:00 to 21:00 inside the town, and then it's 9:00 again, the same
  Tuesday. Everyone does exactly what they did yesterday: the baker drops the same tray, the town crier announces the
  same lost cat, the same cart loses the same wheel at the same corner.
- **Only you remember.** Each loop you keep what you know (and some items); the town resets. It's a time-loop mystery:
  learn the schedule, be in the right place at the right minute, change one thing, see what changes.
- **The Clock Tower** in the square has no hands. The town's Wednesday was locked inside it.

## The quest line (main campaign: Chapter Two)

1. **Slightly Used** — get the Tuesday from the Night Merchant, use it at dusk.
2. **Same Again** — meet the people and notice the loop; the Town Crier, **Barnaby Hollow**, is the only other person who
   half-remembers ("Have we met? I feel we've met. I feel we've met forty thousand times.").
3. **The Schedule** — over several loops, map the day: who's where, when. A journal page fills in as you learn it
   (a real in-game schedule you can read).
4. **Three Small Changes** — the Wednesday is held shut by three things that happen every Tuesday: stop the cart losing its
   wheel (so the doctor arrives), get the lost cat home before the crier announces it (so the crier has nothing to say at
   noon), and make sure the baker's tray doesn't fall (so the bread goes to the tower keeper). Each one needs the right
   item, place and minute.
5. **The Clock Tower** — on the loop where all three change, the tower opens: a dungeon of gears and frozen minutes, with
   the Hunger's **Clock-Eaters** (they eat seconds) and, at the top, the boss **Last Tuesday**.
6. **Wind the Clock** — three endings: give the town its Wednesday (time moves on; it rejoins the world, and the Hunger
   notices); leave it in its perfect Tuesday, but free Barnaby from remembering; or take the Tuesday with you (an item
   that lets you replay one day, a powerful reward at a price).

## Also in the chapter

- Time-loop mechanics in the engine: a "loop region" whose clock resets and whose state (doors, people, items) resets with
  it, while the player's knowledge flags carry over.
- New rewards for builds: a spell (*Rewind*: undo the last few seconds of damage), a weapon that's stronger the more often
  you've repeated the day, an accessory that slows enemies at noon.
- Companions react to the loop (Baggy loves it: "I've solved this case eleven times!"; Doobie keeps finding the same
  crumb).
- Side tales: the cat (Mister Pemberton, who is not lost, only very busy); a duel the same two people have fought
  forty thousand times and neither has ever won.
- Bestiary entries, self-tests of a whole loop, balance checks, screenshots, as usual.
