THE UNCOUNTED
A voice guide for Coth and Eranu

Hi Coth, hi Eranu!

You two are in the game as yourselves, and this file is everything you need to
give those characters your real voices: who they are, how the lines should
feel, how to record them, and every line with its file name.

Nothing here is a rule. If a line wants to go somewhere funnier or more
natural, follow it. You know these two characters better than anyone.

In total: 62 lines just for Eranu, 70 just for Coth, plus 23 you share in
scenes together.

==============================================================================
1. THE STORY YOU'RE IN
==============================================================================

You only need the side quest called "Cothepoo, Come Home".

Eranu sits in the window seat of the Lantern Inn, watching the door. Three
days ago her waifu, Coth, went out to find some trousers and never came back.
She asks the player to fetch him.

The player finds Coth in the Whistling Wood, camped with Sir Pebbleton, a
round stone with a face painted on it. Coth is completely certain he has been
lost for a thousand years. It has been three days. Tally marks on a tree prove
it: 72 hours. His enormous beard, it turns out, was always like that.

He goes home. He bursts into the inn. Eranu crosses the room in four strides,
looks like she might hit him, and hugs him off the floor instead. Marta brings
two bowls of stew. Somebody starts a slow clap.

After that, both of them can join the player's party and walk the world,
chatting, fighting and teasing each other.


==============================================================================
2. PLAYING THEM TOGETHER
==============================================================================

THE MAIN THING: play it straight. These characters never know they are funny.
Coth truly believes it has been a thousand years. Eranu truly thinks "waifu"
is a normal word that everybody knows. Skip the wink at the audience and the
jokes get bigger.

UNDER THE JOKE: she was scared, and he was lonely. They would both rather die
than say so. A few lines let that show for a moment. Those are marked in the
notes. Keep them small, and they will be the best moments you record.

THE RHYTHM: Eranu is calm and speaks in short, level sentences. Coth swings
between grand speeches and tiny defeated mumbles. She wins every exchange, and
she barely has to raise her voice to do it.


==============================================================================
3. PLAYING ERANU
==============================================================================

WHO SHE IS: a tall, confident warrior from the Glens, in a green kilt, with a
round shield and a sword across her knees. She hits things that need hitting
and reads things that need reading. Dry, unimpressed, secretly soft about
exactly one person.

YOUR VOICE: speak low and unhurried. She is never the one in a hurry, and she
gets quieter when she means it. Save real volume for the fights and for one
word: "COTHEPOO."

THE SCOTS: the spelling is only there to help. It was written to be easy to
read in subtitles, so if a word feels wrong in your mouth, say it the way you
would really say it. Your natural speech beats anything on this page. If you
change a word a lot, tell BadSad so the subtitle can be changed to match.

WORDS IN HER LINES
  aye = yes               ken = know             dinnae = don't
  naw = no                no' = not              doesnae = doesn't
  whit = what             wee = small            ma = my, or Mum in "Ma's"
  ye / yer = you / your   hame = home            oot / doon = out / down
  guid = good             braw = fine, great     daft = silly
  numpty / daftie = idiot, said with love        havering = talking nonsense
  gie = give              haud = hold            deid = dead
  fae / tae = from / to   aw = all               masel' = myself
  windae = window         hoose = house          auld = old
  kirk = church           haar = sea fog         mind = remember, or watch out
  targe = her round shield


==============================================================================
4. PLAYING COTH
==============================================================================

WHO HE IS: skinny, scruffy, with a beard to his belt and hair longer still. No
trousers, ever, in any weather. There are two Coths in this script, and the
difference is what makes him work.

COTH IN THE WOOD (the castaway): wide-eyed and hoarse, with too much dignity
for a man with no trousers. He has had nobody to talk to but a rock for three
days, and it has gone straight to his head. Big, formal, theatrical speech, as
if he is reciting his memoirs. He loves that rock, and that love is real.

COTH AT HOME (the companion): cheerful, a bit unhinged, proud of his legs.
Still insists on the thousand years, but now it's more of a hobby. Easy,
happy, usually eating something.

YOUR VOICE: talk as yourself and turn it up. No accent is needed. Go big when
he is proud, and go tiny when Eranu looks at him. That drop is his best joke:
he can go from "A free man of the wilds" to "...Your waifu" in a single
breath.

SIR PEBBLETON: always speak of him with complete respect. He is a person. He
is the quiet one.


==============================================================================
5. RECORDING
==============================================================================

GEAR: a phone or a headset is fine. No studio needed.

ROOM: somewhere quiet and soft. A wardrobe full of clothes is a famous trick.
Turn off fans and close the window.

MIC: about a hand's width from your mouth and slightly to one side, so the P's
in "Pebbleton" and "poo" don't pop. For shouts, lean back or turn away a
little so the sound doesn't distort.

TAKES: do two or three of anything you care about and keep the best. Leave
half a second of silence at the end of each line.

KEEP IT EVEN: try to record in one sitting, or at least in the same spot at
the same distance, so the character doesn't change sound between lines.

FILE TYPE: the game plays .ogg, .wav and .mp3 only. Mono, 44.1 or 48 kHz.
Watch out: many phones save .m4a, which the game can't play. The free program
Audacity can convert it, or just send the files to BadSad as they are.

FILE NAMES: each line has its own name, shown above it. For example, the line
marked [bark.eranu.1] is saved as bark.eranu.1.ogg. Spell it exactly, dots
included. Lines nobody has recorded just show as subtitles, so you can do a
few at a time and nothing breaks.

THE LAZY WAY: if naming files sounds awful, record one long file. Say the name
of each line out loud, leave a pause, then read the line. Send that to BadSad.


==============================================================================
6. HOW TO READ THE SCRIPT
==============================================================================

[some.file.name]   The file name for the line underneath it.

[beat]             A pause. Something happens here in the game: the player
                   reads a bit of description, or the character does something.
                   Leave about a second and carry on in the same recording. The
                   note usually says what is happening.

CAPITALS           Lean on that word. It doesn't always mean louder.

...                Trailing off, or working up to saying something.

Note:              A suggestion about how the line could feel. Ignore freely.

Other people's lines in a scene are shown for context, marked "(not yours)".
Don't record those. Every line in a scene gets its own file, so the game can
play them one after another.

WATCH OUT: scene files are named after whoever starts the scene, not whoever is
talking. Coth's "A free man of the wilds" is saved as
comp.eranu.banter.waifu.1, with "eranu" in the name. That's correct. Just copy
the name as written.

SOME LINES REPEAT: the first line of each conversation plays every time the
player talks to you, so keep those light. Fight lines come up over and over, so
keep them short and punchy, without leaning too hard on the joke.

==============================================================================
7. WHERE TO START
==============================================================================

Short on time? Record in this order. The most important lines come first:

  1. The Reunion (the scene right below this list)
  2. Scenes for Coth and Eranu together
  3. Your conversations
  4. Fight lines and everything else

==============================================================================
8. THE REUNION (both of you)
==============================================================================

This is the peak of the whole story, so give it your best takes. Each page
plays as a full-screen card, and the player reads the description while you
speak. Don't worry about pauses or timing here. Just record the words, and
BadSad can place them.

The last card has both of you on it, but the game plays one file per card.
Record your own line under the name shown, and BadSad will join them into one
file, story.cothepoo.3. If you can record that one together, even better. Do
it, and name the single file story.cothepoo.3.

COTH:
[story.cothepoo.1]
    "I HAVE RETURNED [beat] AFTER A THOUSAND YEARS."
    Note: Door flies open. He has rehearsed this. Announce it to the whole
          inn, as loudly as you can, with a proud pause at the [beat].

ERANU:
[story.cothepoo.2]
    "COTHEPOO. Three days, ya absolute numpty. Three DAYS."
    Note: Everything she's been holding in for three days. 'COTHEPOO' is the
          one really big shout she gets, right into his ear. Then it starts to
          crack. She's still holding him off the floor.

COTH:
[story.cothepoo.3.coth]
    "Felt like a thousand"
    Note: Muffled, into her shoulder. A hand over your mouth or your face in
          your sleeve works. All the theatre is gone. This is the truest thing
          he says.

ERANU:
[story.cothepoo.3.eranu]
    "Aye [beat] It did, ya daftie."
    Note: Quiet, and nothing like the shout before it. He just said it felt
          like a thousand years, and she's agreeing: it did for her too. Let
          her be soft for exactly this long.

10. ERANU'S LINES
==============================================================================


BARKS
-----
Muttered while the player walks past, to nobody in particular. Keep it low and
half to yourself. Nobody is meant to answer.

[bark.eranu.1]
    "Three days. Three DAYS."
    Note: To herself. It's the grievance she has been chewing on for days.
          Lean on the second DAYS.

[bark.eranu.2]
    "Have ye seen a man wi' nae trousers? Ye'd ken."
    Note: Asking a stranger, completely matter of fact. This is a normal thing
          to ask.

[bark.eranu.3]
    "Aye, he's back. Dinnae tell him I was worried."
    Note: After he's home. Low and sideways, like a secret. A small smile you
          can hear.


CONVERSATION
------------
The player is standing right in front of you. Only your spoken words are
listed; the description around them appears on screen.

[dlg.eranu.root]
    "Aye? Whit is it?"
    Note: This plays every time. One eye on the player, one on the door. She's
          listening, but only just.

[dlg.eranu.who]
    "Eranu. Fae the Glens, up north, where the weather has opinions an' shares
    them. [beat] I hit things that need hittin' and I read things that need
    readin'. Folk roond here are aye surprised I dae both. Then they're
    surprised again."
    Note: [beat]: she pats her sword. 'Then they're surprised again' is a
          friendly warning.

[dlg.eranu.waiting]
    "I'm waitin' on ma waifu. [beat] Coth. Tall, skinny, beard doon tae here,
    hair doon tae there. Nae trousers, usually. Ye'd mind him. [beat] Three
    days ago he said he was awa' oot tae find some trousers. He's no' come
    back. Coth doesnae find trousers. Trousers find Coth, an' he runs."
    Note: Their big introduction. Say 'ma waifu' with complete confidence, as
          though everyone knows the word. [beat]s: she lets that land, then
          frowns at the door. Finish deadpan: 'Trousers find Coth, an' he
          runs' is simply a fact.

[dlg.eranu.waifu]
    "Aye. Ma waifu. [beat] He protests. He's wrang. [beat] So. Are ye gonnae
    find him or no'?"
    Note: She looks straight at the player, daring them to say something. 'He
          protests. He's wrang.' Case closed. Then right back to business.

[dlg.eranu.accept]
    "Guid. [beat] He went west, intae the Whistling Wood. He'll have made camp
    somewhere daft, he'll have made a pal oot o' somethin' that's no' alive,
    an' he'll have lost count o' the days. He aye loses count o' the days.
    [beat] Bring him hame. Tell him Eranu says come hame, Cothepoo."
    Note: 'Guid' is one word and a nod. [beat]: she stands up, taller than
          expected. She knows him so well that the list comes easily. SOFTER
          ON THE LAST LINE: 'Tell him Eranu says come hame, Cothepoo' is the
          first crack in the armour. Barely let it show.

[dlg.eranu.news]
    "West, in the Whistling Wood. Look for a camp somewhere daft, a wee fire,
    an' a beard. [beat] An' if he tells ye it's been a thousand years, he's
    havering. He'll have been countin' somethin'. Make him count it oot loud."
    Note: Worry disguised as instructions. [beat]: her grip tightens on the
          sword.

[dlg.eranu.nickname]
    "Because he's Coth, an' because he's a wee poo sometimes. [beat] He hates
    it. That's how ye ken it's love."
    Note: Her mouth twitches: she's almost smiling and refuses to. 'That's how
          ye ken it's love' is dry and completely sincere.

[dlg.eranu.reunion]
    "His [beat] I've carried them since he left, for when we found him. He
    says he doesnae want them back. [beat] An' this, for yer trouble. [beat]
    If ye ever need a sword arm, or two, ye ken where the windae seat is."
    Note: Right after the big hug, and she has got herself back under control.
          'His' is one quiet word as she hands over the folded trousers.
          Second [beat]: Coth yells 'I DON'T' behind her, and she ignores him.
          Third [beat]: she hands over a purse. Warm by the end.

[dlg.eranu.join]
    "Aye, go on, then. Somebody's got tae keep ye alive. [beat] Dinnae wander
    aff, Cothepoo."
    Note: [beat]: she glances across the table. 'Dinnae wander aff, Cothepoo'
          is half threat, half love.

[dlg.eranu.full]
    "Ye've a full party already. I'll no' be the spare wheel on anybody's
    cart."

[dlg.eranu.wait]
    "Aye. I'll be at the windae."
    Note: Quiet. She's already heading back to her seat, and her eyes go to
          the door out of habit.


WALKING AND CHATTING
--------------------
Random chatter while walking. Say it offhand, as if the thought just wandered
in. Silence is normal between these.

[comp.eranu.idle.1]
    "Keep yer guard up. Naw, higher. Aye, that's it."
    Note: Coaching the player, without looking up. The 'Aye' is approval and
          costs her something.

[comp.eranu.idle.2]
    "Back hame, this'd count as a braw day. Back hame, the rain comes
    sideways."

[comp.eranu.idle.3]
    "I read once this road was built by a king who wanted tae see the sea. He
    never did. The road did."

[comp.eranu.idle.4]
    "If anythin' jumps oot at us, you go left, I go through it."

[comp.eranu.idle.5]
    "Cothepoo, stop lickin' that mushroom."
    Note: No surprise at all. A woman who has said this many times before.

[comp.eranu.idle.6]
    "Dunstan, lift wi' yer legs. Yer LEGS, lad."
    Note: Called across a distance, like a tired PE teacher. Lean on LEGS.

[comp.eranu.idle.7]
    "Baggy, ye've had that magnifyin' glass on ma boots for a full minute."


IN A FIGHT
----------
Shouted mid-fight, often. Short, with full energy from the belly. Back off the
mic.

[comp.eranu.combat.1]
    "Come on, then!"

[comp.eranu.combat.2]
    "Get behind the shield!"

[comp.eranu.combat.3]
    "Is that aw ye've got?"

[comp.eranu.combat.4]
    "For the Glens!"
    Note: Full war cry. This is the one to really let go on.


AFTER A KILL
------------
Right after an enemy falls. You're catching your breath and pleased with
yourself.

[comp.eranu.kill.1]
    "Next."
    Note: Bored. One word, already looking for the next enemy.

[comp.eranu.kill.2]
    "Aye. Stay doon."

[comp.eranu.kill.3]
    "That's for Cothepoo, whatever ye did tae him."
    Note: A little joke to herself. She has no idea if it did anything to him.
          Doesn't matter.

[comp.eranu.kill.4]
    "Braw."
    Note: One satisfied word. Like the last bite of something good.


HEALING YOU
-----------
You're patching the player up, close by and a little distracted, hands busy.

[comp.eranu.heal.1]
    "Here. Drink this an' stop bleedin' on ma kilt."

[comp.eranu.heal.2]

    Note: Gruff, and it's how she says she cares. 'Get that doon ye' like a
